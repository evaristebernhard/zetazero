\begin{abstract}
We construct a finite-dimensional Hermitian contour form from an analytic Hardy
gauge and a near-lattice packet family.  On the zero side, stationary residues
convert off-critical-line geometry into negative inertia.  After the generic
real-symmetric perturbation used to remove stationary multiplicities, this
inertia has an exact scalar re-encoding: the local principal part
\(M_\Omega=\operatorname{PP}_{\Omega}(H/H')\) has a generalized-Nevanlinna
kernel with exactly \(n_-(A_H)\) negative squares; its Cayley transform is a
generalized Schur function whose Kre\u{\i}n--Langer denominator has the same
degree, and this degree is equivalently the number of upper-half-plane zeros
of a polynomial \(P_\Omega+iQ_\Omega\), or the upper-half-plane eigenvalue
count of a diagonal matrix plus a rank-one perturbation.  This rewrite
identifies the integer defect but does not by itself estimate it.

On the arithmetic side the exact contour matrix is retained as the coupled
four-block sum
\[
A_F=A_T^{\rm true}+L_T^{\rm geom}+J_T^{\rm arch}+R_T^{\rm exact}.
\]
The complete non-\(f'\) Dirichlet source
\(-P(s)+Q(s)/(f(s)-P(s))\) remains inside the theorem-facing operator, the
Hermitian archimedean self block is not discarded by holomorphy, and the exact
fourth block contains the \(f'\) and horizontal contributions.  Finite
computations show that the inertia of partial block sums can differ sharply
from that of the complete contour matrix, so no individual block is assigned
an independent positivity interpretation.

We prove a conditional density-one theorem.  If the negative part of the
relative defect between the true one-\(\chi\) operator and a positive reference
model has normalized Hilbert--Schmidt square \(O((\log T)^{-2})\), and the
geometric edge and regular remainders satisfy the stated rank and relative
Hilbert--Schmidt estimates, then
\[
\frac{N_{0,T}}{N_T}=1-O((\log T)^{-2})-o(1)=1-o(1).
\]
The negative-square/determinant rewrite therefore supplies a second exact
description of the zero-side defect; an unconditional argument would still
have to control that defect from the complete coupled right-edge arithmetic
representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main theorem and notation}

Let
\[
N_T:=N(T,2T)
\]
denote the number, with multiplicity, of nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ with
imaginary part in $[T,2T]$, and let $N_{0,T}$ denote the number of those zeros
on the critical line. Thus $N_T\asymp T\log T$. Put
\[
L:=\log\frac{T}{2\pi}.
\]
Throughout the paper the certified reference-floor constant is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\kappa_*:=0.02.}
\label{eq:global-kappa}
\end{equation}

For a Hermitian operator $B$, write $B_-=(-B)_+$ for its negative part.  Let
$A_T^{\rm true}$ be the retained log-only Hermitian operator defined in
Section~\ref{sec:model-floor}; in particular, its physical stationary carrier
$e(-ne^d)$ is not discarded.  Let $P_T$ be the positive reference model and
$\Gfr_T$ its reference Gram.  For a deterministic sequence $\lambda_T>0$ put
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal D_T:=\Gfr_T^{-1/2}
 (A_T^{\rm true}-\lambda_TP_T)\Gfr_T^{-1/2},
\qquad
\beta_-(T;\lambda_T):=
\frac{\tr[(\mathcal D_T)_-^2]}
{(\kappa_*\lambda_T)^2N_T}.}
\label{eq:beta-minus-intro}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional density one from the one-$\chi$ defect]
\label{thm:main}
Assume that the reference model satisfies
\[
P_T\succeq\kappa_*\,\Gfr_T
\]
on the retained space and that, for some deterministic $\lambda_T$ satisfying
$\lambda_T\ge\lambda_*>0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\beta_-(T;\lambda_T)=O(L^{-2}).}
\label{eq:beta-minus-hypothesis-intro}
\end{equation}
The exact contour matrix has the four-block decomposition
\[
A_F=A_T^{\rm true}+L_T^{\rm geom}+J_T^{\rm arch}+R_T^{\rm exact}
\]
of Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-four-block-decomposition}. Assume also that
on the retained space $L_T^{\rm geom}+J_T^{\rm arch}
=E_T^{\rm geom}+R_T^{\rm edge+arch}$, where
$\rank E_T^{\rm geom}=o(N_T)$ and
\begin{equation}
\left\|\Gfr_T^{-1/2}R_T^\nu\Gfr_T^{-1/2}\right\|_{S_2}^2=o(N_T)
\quad(\nu\in\{\mathrm{edge+arch},f',\mathrm{hor}\}),
\label{eq:regular-lambda-scale-intro}
\end{equation}
Here $R_T^{\rm exact}=R_T^{f'}+R_T^{\rm hor}$ and
$R_T^{\rm reg}=R_T^{\rm edge+arch}+R_T^{f'}+R_T^{\rm hor}$.
Then, as $T\to\infty$,
\[
\boxed{\frac{N_{0,T}}{N_T}=1-O(L^{-2})-o(1)=1-o(1)}.
\]
Consequently, by dyadic summation,
\[
\boxed{\frac{N_0(T)}{N(T)}\longrightarrow1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Status of the arithmetic decomposition]
The four-block identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-four-block-decomposition}
is exact. The relative estimate in \eqref{eq:regular-lambda-scale-intro} for
$R_T^{\rm edge+arch}$ has not been derived from the contour. In particular,
$J_T^{\rm arch}=I_f+I_f^*$ survives the holomorphic identity
\eqref{eq:holomorphic-self-elimination}. A separate estimate or a revised
principal operator is needed to establish the theorem's quantitative hypotheses.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scale of the regular remainder]
The Hilbert--Schmidt triangle inequality and $\lambda_T\ge\lambda_*>0$ turn
the three estimates in \eqref{eq:regular-lambda-scale-intro} into
$\|\Gfr_T^{-1/2}R_T^{\rm reg}\Gfr_T^{-1/2}\|_{S_2}^2
=o(\lambda_T^2N_T)$.  Thus the scaled bound is a conclusion, not an additional
hypothesis.  Since $L\to\infty$, the bound
$\beta_-(T;\lambda_T)=O(L^{-2})$ is in particular $o(1)$.
\end{remark}

Hardy's theorem established infinitely many zeros on the critical line, and
Selberg proved that a positive proportion lie there
\cite{Hardy1914,Selberg1942,Titchmarsh}.  The modern quantitative line begins
with Levinson's mollifier method and its later refinements
\cite{Levinson1974,Young2010,Conrey1989,BCY2011,Feng2012,
PrattRobles2018,PrattRoblesZaharescuZeindler2020}.  Closely related work on
simple and distinct zeros, pair correlation, and finite-dimensional positivity
includes
\cite{HeathBrown1979,Anderson1983,Montgomery1973,ConreyGhoshGonek1998,
BuiHeathBrown2013,ChirreGoncalvesDeLaat2020,Aryan2022,
BaluyotGoldstonSuriajayaTurnage2024,GoldstonSuriajaya2025,
GoldstonSuriajaya2026,AlpogeFurman2026}.  In particular, Alp\"oge and Furman
obtained an unconditional proportion exceeding two thirds for zeros that are
both simple and on the critical line by combining a finite compression of a
Hermitian zero form with inertia.

A second strand concerns the size and statistics of $\zeta$, its derivatives,
and the Hardy $Z$-function.  Mean-value and discrete-moment methods were developed
through the work of Gonek, Conrey--Ghosh, Conrey--Ghosh--Gonek, Ng,
Garunk\v{s}tis--Steuding, Hughes, Milinovich, and later authors
\cite{Gonek1984,ConreyGhosh1985,ConreyGhosh1989,Conrey1988,
ConreyGhosh1998,GarunkstisSteuding2005,Ng2007,Hughes2002,
Milinovich2010,Milinovich2011,HughesMartinPearceCrump2024,
HughesPearceCrump2025,PearceCrump2025,HLP}.  These results are especially close
to the present zero-side use of stationary points of the Hardy gauge and to the
right-edge expansion through $Z_1'/Z_1$.

There is also an older positivity and inertia viewpoint.  Yoshida studied
Hermitian forms attached to zeta functions, and Bombieri analyzed finite
truncations of Weil's quadratic functional; under a finiteness assumption on
off-critical zeros, Bombieri's truncations eventually have exactly one
negative eigenvalue for each reflected off-line pair
\cite{Yoshida1992,Bombieri2000}.  Generalized Nevanlinna and Schur theory
provides the abstract language of finite negative squares and finite Blaschke
defects used in Section~\ref{sec:negative-square-rewrite}
\cite{Lilleberg2020}.  In the present argument this language is applied only
to the finite rational principal part determined by stationary poles inside a
single good rectangle; no global generalized-Nevanlinna property of
\(F/F'\) is assumed.

The arithmetic side also intersects the extensive literature on twisted moments,
Dirichlet-polynomial mean values, mollifiers, and ratios of $L$-functions
\cite{BalasubramanianConreyHeathBrown1985,Farmer1993,
DeshouillersIwaniec1987,BettinChandee2018,BettinChandeeRadziwill2017,
BettinGonek2017,ConreyFarmerKeatingRubinsteinSnaith2003,CFKRS2005,
ConreyFarmerZirnbauer2008,ConreySnaith2007,ConreyGonek2001,
ConreyIwaniecSoundararajan2013,Sono2018,HLZ}.  Adjacent work on zero gaps,
derivative zeros, and critical-line geometry includes
\cite{Conrey1983,Bui2011,BuiMilinovichNg2010,BuiMilinovich2018,
FengWu2012,ChaubeyMalikRoblesZaharescu2018,Ivic1977,KeatingSnaith2000,
Kirila2018,Odlyzko1987,RudnickSarnak1996}.  The argument below uses a different
assembly: the same Hardy-gauge contour matrix is represented on the zero side
and on the packetized right edge, the true log-only operator is compared
spectrally with a positive reference model, and the remaining terms are
separated into low-rank and relative Hilbert--Schmidt classes.

The proof is organized around one normalized contour matrix.  Section
\ref{sec:zero-side} identifies its stationary residue blocks.  Section
\ref{sec:negative-square-rewrite} then rewrites the generic zero-side defect
exactly as a finite principal-part negative-square index, a Kre\u{\i}n--Langer
Blaschke degree, and a rank-one determinant half-plane count.  This rewrite is
diagnostic rather than eliminative: it identifies the integer defect but does
not estimate it.  Sections~\ref{sec:right-edge} and following return to the
same unperturbed contour matrix from the arithmetic side.  The exact four-block
sum is therefore the mechanism available to control the defect, not a
collection in which all but \(A_T^{\rm true}\) may be discarded.

The holomorphic identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:holomorphic-self-elimination}
cancels only the anti-Hermitian vertical $f$ contribution.  The conditional
theorem compares the true packet operator with the positive reference model
through the one-sided defect \eqref{eq:beta-minus-intro}; no scalar diagonal or
lagwise packet--HLZ reduction is used.  The complete source containing
$\Delta H_{\rm dir}$ is absorbed into $A_T^{\rm true}$ before stationary
normalization; safe-line smallness is not used as an operator estimate.  The
superseded frozen-self, critical-$J$ AFE, one-sided common-output,
HLP-resummation, and macroscopic-shift source files are excluded from the
active manuscript.  Appendix~\ref{sec:legacy-route-note} records only their
historical scope; the detailed earlier text remains recoverable from the Git
history.
