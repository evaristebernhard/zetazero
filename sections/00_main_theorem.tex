\begin{abstract}
We construct a finite-dimensional Hermitian contour form from an analytic Hardy
gauge and a near-lattice packet family. Its zero-side residue representation
converts off-critical-line stationary geometry into negative inertia. On the
arithmetic side we split the exact right-edge source, on its original
absolute-convergence line, into a finite completed model and a direct
exact--model residual.  The finite model is evaluated by the critical-$J$
identity together with the scalar Heap--Li--Zhao three-$Z$ diagonal calculation
for its logarithmic-derivative faces; the packet lift of the scalar three-$Z$
identity is obtained from the exact one-sided packet factorization and Fubini.
The HLZ Mellin scale is changed to the local physical height by a
residue-preserving auxiliary-weight rescaling, while the two smoothed
critical-$J$ approximate-functional-equation halves are transferred to the
sharp full fibre with an explicit regular error.  The residual keeps its
physical one-$\chi$ carrier and is separated into low-sideband edge terms and
high-sideband regular terms.  Positivity is imposed only on the fully frozen
completed lag operator; all finite-$T$ height, self-factor, and endpoint
deformations are kept on their physical carrier channel and placed in the
relative Hilbert--Schmidt remainder.  A direct Schur estimate gives the frozen
operator a uniform spectral lower bound on the mean-zero fibres. The stationary
edge contribution has rank $o(N_T)$, while the regular remainder is $o(N_T)$ in
the relative Hilbert--Schmidt metric. A finite-dimensional inertia argument then implies
that all but $o(N_T)$ zeros in a dyadic interval lie on the critical line.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main theorem and notation}

Let
\[
N_T:=N(T,2T)
\]
denote the number, with multiplicity, of nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ with
imaginary part in $[T,2T]$, and let $N_{0,T}$ denote the number of those zeros
on the critical line. Thus $N_T\asymp T\log T$. Put
\[
L:=\log\frac{T}{2\pi}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Density one on the critical line]
\label{thm:main}
As $T\to\infty$,
\[
\boxed{\frac{N_{0,T}}{N_T}=1-o(1)}.
\]
Consequently, by dyadic summation,
\[
\boxed{\frac{N_0(T)}{N(T)}\longrightarrow1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Hardy's theorem established infinitely many zeros on the critical line, and
Selberg proved that a positive proportion lie there
\cite{Hardy1914,Selberg1942,Titchmarsh}.  The modern quantitative line begins
with Levinson's mollifier method and its later refinements
\cite{Levinson1974,Young2010,Conrey1989,BCY2011,Feng2012,
PrattRobles2018,PrattRoblesZaharescuZeindler2020}.  Closely related work on
simple and distinct zeros, pair correlation, and finite-dimensional positivity
includes
\cite{HeathBrown1979,Anderson1983,Montgomery1973,ConreyGhoshGonek1998,
BuiHeathBrown2013,ChirreGoncalvesDeLaat2020,Aryan2022,
BaluyotGoldstonSuriajayaTurnage2024,GoldstonSuriajaya2025,
GoldstonSuriajaya2026,AlpogeFurman2026}.  In particular, Alp\"oge and Furman
obtained an unconditional proportion exceeding two thirds for zeros that are
both simple and on the critical line by combining a finite compression of a
Hermitian zero form with inertia.

A second strand concerns the size and statistics of $\zeta$, its derivatives,
and the Hardy $Z$-function.  Mean-value and discrete-moment methods were developed
through the work of Gonek, Conrey--Ghosh, Conrey--Ghosh--Gonek, Ng,
Garunk\v{s}tis--Steuding, Hughes, Milinovich, and later authors
\cite{Gonek1984,ConreyGhosh1985,ConreyGhosh1989,Conrey1988,
ConreyGhosh1998,GarunkstisSteuding2005,Ng2007,Hughes2002,
Milinovich2010,Milinovich2011,HughesMartinPearceCrump2024,
HughesPearceCrump2025,PearceCrump2025,HLP}.  These results are especially close
to the present zero-side use of stationary points of the Hardy gauge and to the
right-edge expansion through $Z_1'/Z_1$.

The arithmetic side also intersects the extensive literature on twisted moments,
Dirichlet-polynomial mean values, mollifiers, and ratios of $L$-functions
\cite{BalasubramanianConreyHeathBrown1985,Farmer1993,
DeshouillersIwaniec1987,BettinChandee2018,BettinChandeeRadziwill2017,
BettinGonek2017,ConreyFarmerKeatingRubinsteinSnaith2003,CFKRS2005,
ConreyFarmerZirnbauer2008,ConreySnaith2007,ConreyGonek2001,
ConreyIwaniecSoundararajan2013,Sono2018,HLZ}.  Adjacent work on zero gaps,
derivative zeros, and critical-line geometry includes
\cite{Conrey1983,Bui2011,BuiMilinovichNg2010,BuiMilinovich2018,
FengWu2012,ChaubeyMalikRoblesZaharescu2018,Ivic1977,KeatingSnaith2000,
Kirila2018,Odlyzko1987,RudnickSarnak1996}.  The argument below uses a different
assembly: the same Hardy-gauge contour matrix is represented on the zero side
and on the packetized right edge, its principal lag operator is given a uniform
spectral floor, and the remaining terms are separated into low-rank and relative
Hilbert--Schmidt classes.

The proof is organized around the normalized contour matrix introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:common-matrix}, whose zero-side and arithmetic-side
representations are developed in the subsequent sections.  The classical HLZ
input is used only in its scalar finite-model form, with the auxiliary function
chosen on separated Cauchy contours.  The packet lift of this scalar main term
and its scalar contour error is obtained from the exact one-sided packet
factorization and Fubini in Lemma~\ref{lem:model-HLZ-Fubini-lift}.  The exact HLP
resolvent is never required to satisfy a packet-valued HLZ theorem: after the
exact source split \eqref{eq:direct-model-source-split} it remains in the direct
one-$\chi$ sideband decomposition.  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:packet-bilinear-HLZ} is
retained later only to record a stronger optional comparison route and is not
used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
