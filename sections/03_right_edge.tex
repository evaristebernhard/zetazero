\section{Right-edge arithmetic source and exact HLP algebra}
\label{sec:right-edge}

\subsection{Reflected archimedean contribution}
Differentiate the functional equation $\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)$. Since
$f(s)=-\frac12\chi'/\chi(s)$ and $f(s)=f(1-s)$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Z_1(s)=-\chi(s)Z_1(1-s).}
\label{eq:Z1-FE}
\end{equation}
Taking logarithmic derivatives gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
L_1(s)+L_1(1-s)=\frac{\chi'}{\chi}(s),
\qquad L_1:=\frac{Z_1'}{Z_1}.
}
\label{eq:Z1-log-FE}
\end{equation}
The two vertical sides are related by the Hermitian reflection
$\iota(s):=1-\bar s$, which preserves positive height.  The holomorphic map
$s\mapsto1-s$ alone reverses the sign of the height and is not used as a
geometric contour substitution.  We keep the complex conjugation in the
reflection formula explicitly.  Conjugation symmetry and
\eqref{eq:Z1-log-FE} give
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
-L_1(1-\bar s)
=\overline{\,L_1(s)-\frac{\chi'}{\chi}(s)\,}.
}
\label{eq:L1-hermitian-reflection}
\end{equation}
Thus one must not replace $1-\bar s$ by $1-s$ at the scalar level.

The cancellation becomes transparent only for the complete Hermitian
integrand.  Put
\[
H(s):=f(s)+L_1(s)=\frac{\mathscr Z''}{\mathscr Z'}(s).
\]
Differentiating \eqref{eq:hardy-gauge-reflection} gives
\[
\mathscr Z'(\iota(s))=-\overline{\mathscr Z'(s)},
\qquad
\mathscr Z''(\iota(s))=\overline{\mathscr Z''(s)},
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{H(\iota(s))=-\overline{H(s)}.}
\label{eq:hardy-source-adjoint}
\end{equation}
The same reflection law gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
C_{\mathscr Z}^{(p)}(\iota(s))
=\overline{C_{\mathscr Z}^{(p)}(s)},
\qquad
\mathcal B_{v,w}(\iota(s))
=\overline{\mathcal B_{w,v}(s)}.
}
\label{eq:curvature-packet-adjoint}
\end{equation}
Indeed $z(\iota(s))=\overline{z(s)}$, and for a real shift $a$ the whole
shifted Hardy product satisfies
\[
\mathscr Z(\iota(s)+a)\mathscr Z(\iota(s)-a)
=\overline{\mathscr Z(s+a)\mathscr Z(s-a)};
\]
therefore the same identity survives the curvature extraction at $a=0$.

Let $\Gamma_c^+$ denote the right vertical side oriented upward and let
$\Gamma_{1-c}^-$ denote the left vertical side with its boundary orientation,
namely downward.  The reflection $\iota$ maps the underlying right segment onto
the underlying left segment but reverses these chosen orientations.  Define
\[
\mathfrak I_H(v,w)
:=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_c^+}
H(s)C_{\mathscr Z}^{(p)}(s)\mathcal B_{v,w}(s)\,ds,
\qquad
\mathfrak I_{\rm left}(v,w)
:=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{1-c}^-}
H(s)C_{\mathscr Z}^{(p)}(s)\mathcal B_{v,w}(s)\,ds.
\]
Then parametrizing $s=c+it$ and using
\eqref{eq:hardy-source-adjoint}--\eqref{eq:curvature-packet-adjoint} gives the
exact matrix-adjoint identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathfrak I_{\rm left}(v,w)
=\overline{\mathfrak I_H(w,v)}
=\mathfrak I_H^*(v,w).
}
\label{eq:right-left-matrix-adjoint}
\end{equation}
The minus sign in \eqref{eq:hardy-source-adjoint} is exactly cancelled by the
reversed orientation of the left boundary.

Now expand $H=f+L_1$ only after this matrix identity has been established.
Writing $I_{\rm right}$ for the right-edge form with scalar source $L_1$ and
\[
J_{\rm arch}:=I_f+I_f^*,
\]
where $I_f$ is the same right-edge form with scalar source $f$, the two vertical
sides contribute exactly
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
I_{\rm right}+I_{\rm right}^*+J_{\rm arch},
\qquad
2f=-\frac{\chi'}{\chi}.
}
\label{eq:J-I-source-split}
\end{equation}
Thus $J_{\rm arch}$ consists of one $f$-half on each adjoint orientation; in the
combined Hermitian bookkeeping this is precisely the full archimedean self
source $2f=-\chi'/\chi$.  The final decomposition is therefore unchanged, but
its justification is now matrix-level rather than a scalar shortcut that
obscures the conjugation and adjoint structure.  Here $I_{\rm right}$ contains all stationary arithmetic
information. No $L_1$ contour is pushed through an interior stationary zero.
The separate archimedean self source is holomorphic in the high rectangle and
may be moved by Cauchy's theorem.  Equivalently, each completed right-edge
orientation carries the scalar source $f+L_1$.

For bounded normalized curvature $p$, put $a=p/L$ and let
$\mathcal B_{v,w}(s)$ denote the holomorphic packet-polarization multiplier.
Set
\[
\mathcal S_a(s)
:=\mathscr Z(s+a)\mathscr Z(s-a)\mathcal B_{v,w}(s),
\qquad
\Phi_J(s;a):=f(s)\mathcal S_a(s)
=-\frac12\frac{\chi'}{\chi}(s)\mathcal S_a(s).
\]
Thus $\Phi_J$ is exactly one half of the full self source
$J_{\rm arch}$ in \eqref{eq:J-I-source-split}.
The shifts $a=O(L^{-1})$ leave the zeta poles at bounded imaginary height, so
$\Phi_J$ is holomorphic on and inside the right half-rectangle
\[
\mathcal R_T^+:=\{\sigma+it:1/2\le\sigma\le c,
\ T_1\le t\le T_2\},
\]
which is the right half of \eqref{eq:symmetric-contour-rectangle}, uniformly
for $|p|\le P$. With the vertical sides oriented upward and the
usual top/bottom orientations, Cauchy's theorem gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\int_{\Gamma_{1/2}^{+}}\Phi_J(s;a)\,ds
=\int_{\Gamma_c^{+}}\Phi_J(s;a)\,ds
+R_{{\rm hor},T}(a),
}
\label{eq:critical-J-move}
\end{equation}
where
\[
R_{{\rm hor},T}(a)
=-\int_{\mathcal H_T^+}\Phi_J(s;a)\,ds
 -\int_{\mathcal H_T^-}\Phi_J(s;a)\,ds.
\]
The reflected Hermitian orientation gives the adjoint identity. Summing the two
orientations forms the Hermitian pair $\mathcal J_f+\mathcal J_f^*$; in the
symmetric source bookkeeping this is one copy of $f$ on each orientation and
therefore reconstructs the full self source $-\chi'/\chi=2f$ in
\eqref{eq:J-I-source-split}, together with the corresponding adjoint pair of
right-edge and horizontal terms. Because all fixed $p$-derivatives remain holomorphic in
the same rectangle,
\begin{equation}
\partial_p^j\mathcal J_{{\rm crit},T}(p)
=\partial_p^j\mathcal J_{{\rm right},T}(p)
 +\partial_p^jR_{{\rm hor},T}(p),
\qquad 0\le j\le2.
\label{eq:critical-J-curvature}
\end{equation}
Thus the critical-$J$ contribution is fixed unambiguously before the common
curvature is taken.

We insert the packet multiplier and the common curvature
\eqref{eq:curvature-product} before deforming the contour to the right edge.
On that edge,
\[
\mathscr Z(s+a)\mathscr Z(s-a)
=\Gamma_a(s)\zeta(s+a)\zeta(s-a),
\]
where
\[
\Gamma_0(s)=\chi(1-s),
\qquad
\partial_a\Gamma_a|_{a=0}=0,
\qquad
\partial_a^2\Gamma_a|_{a=0}=2f'(s)\chi(1-s).
\]
Since $f'(\sigma+it)=O(T^{-1})$ on fixed strips with $t\asymp T$, the last term
is assigned from the start to the regular remainder.

Fix the sign convention
\begin{equation}
P(s):=-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta(s),
\qquad
Q(s):=-P'(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{n^s}
\quad (\Re s>1).
\label{eq:PQ-convention}
\end{equation}
Then the right-edge logarithmic-derivative orientation itself has scalar source
\[
L_1(s)=\frac{Z_1'}{Z_1}(s)
=-P(s)+\frac{f'(s)+Q(s)}{f(s)-P(s)}.
\]
The full archimedean self term in \eqref{eq:J-I-source-split} is $2f$; after the
symmetric half-allocation described above, each completed right-edge orientation
therefore carries $f+L_1$. After separating the $f'$ term, the pointwise
completed source is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\widehat H_{\rm ex}(s)
=f(s)-P(s)+\frac{Q(s)}{f(s)-P(s)}.
}
\label{eq:Hex-variable}
\end{equation}
On a smooth height block with reference height $\tau_b$, put
\begin{equation}
\boxed{F_b:=f(1/2+i\tau_b)\in\mathbb R}
\label{eq:Fb-real-freeze}
\end{equation}
(the reality follows from $|\chi(1/2+it)|=1$ and
$\partial_t\log\chi=i\chi'/\chi$ on the critical line), and define the frozen
source
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_{\rm ex}(s;F_b)=F_b-P(s)+\frac{Q(s)}{F_b-P(s)}.
}
\label{eq:Hex}
\end{equation}
Thus the term $F_b$ is the frozen half of the archimedean self contribution and
is not part of the Dirichlet series for $Z_1'/Z_1$.
The completed two-master model is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
H_{\rm mod}(s;F_b)=F_b-2P(s)+P(s-2/L).
}
\label{eq:Hmod}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Delta H(s;F_b)
=P(s)+\frac{Q(s)}{F_b-P(s)}-P(s-2/L).
}
\label{eq:deltaH}
\end{equation}
This is an exact algebraic identity between the two frozen sources on the
absolute-convergence right edge. The error introduced by replacing the
pointwise source \eqref{eq:Hex-variable} with \eqref{eq:Hex} is estimated below
uniformly over the HLP hierarchy.

\begin{lemma}[Exact curvature Dirichlet source]
\label{lem:curvature-source}
Put
\[
\mathcal C(s):=\zeta(s)^2Q(s).
\]
Then the Hardy-gauge curvature satisfies the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
C_{\mathscr Z}(s)
=q(s)^2\bigl(\mathcal C(s)+f'(s)\zeta(s)^2\bigr),
\qquad q(s)^2=\chi(1-s).
}
\label{eq:curvature-source}
\end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c(n)}{n^s},
\qquad
\boxed{
c(n)=\frac12\sum_{ab=n}\log^2(a/b)\ge0.
}
\label{eq:curvature-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Consequently, after the global normalization $L^{-2}$ already fixed in
Section~\ref{sec:common-matrix}, the principal completed right-edge integrand
has exactly one functional-equation factor $\chi(1-s)$ and scalar Dirichlet
source $\mathcal C(s)H_{\rm ex}(s;F_b)$. Before freezing the slow factor, the
exact completed product separates as
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
q(s)^2\left[
\mathcal C(s)\widehat H_{\rm ex}(s)
+R_{f'}^{\rm src}(s)
\right],
}
\label{eq:exact-fprime-separation}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{f'}^{\rm src}
=f'\zeta^2\widehat H_{\rm ex}
+\frac{f'}{f-P}\mathcal C
+\frac{(f')^2}{f-P}\zeta^2.
}
\label{eq:fprime-source}
\end{equation}
Thus every term containing at least one factor $f'$ belongs exactly once to the
regular $f'$ class, while the principal source contains none.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathscr Z=q\zeta$ and $q'/q=f$,
\[
\mathscr Z''
=q\bigl(\zeta''+2f\zeta'+(f'+f^2)\zeta\bigr).
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
C_{\mathscr Z}
&=\mathscr Z\mathscr Z''-(\mathscr Z')^2\\
&=q^2\bigl(\zeta\zeta''-(\zeta')^2+f'\zeta^2\bigr).
\end{align*}
Because $(\zeta'/\zeta)'=Q$, the first two terms equal
$\zeta^2Q=\mathcal C$, proving \eqref{eq:curvature-source}. Moreover
\eqref{eq:Hex-variable} gives the exact completed scalar factor
\[
f+L_1=\widehat H_{\rm ex}+\frac{f'}{f-P}.
\]
Multiplying this by
$C_{\mathscr Z}/q^2=\mathcal C+f'\zeta^2$ gives
\eqref{eq:exact-fprime-separation}--\eqref{eq:fprime-source}. Finally the
coefficient of $n^{-s}$ in $\zeta\zeta''-(\zeta')^2$ is
\[
\sum_{ab=n}\bigl((\log b)^2-\log a\log b\bigr)
=\frac12\sum_{ab=n}(\log a-\log b)^2,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:curvature-coefficient}.
\end{proof}

From this point the spectral contour variable and the Perron variable are kept
separate. The letter $s=\sigma+it$ is reserved for the high spectral contour.
After stationary phase, the resulting coefficient sequences are represented by
an independent Perron/Mellin variable $z$. We denote their generating
functions by $P(z),Q(z),H_{\rm ex}(z;F_b)$ and $H_{\rm mod}(z;F_b)$ using the
same coefficient formulas as above. All Laurent expansions near $1$ below are
in $z$; no deformation of the original high spectral contour to $s=1$ is ever
performed.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic locator for the translated model scale]
\label{lem:principal-pole-cluster}
Write $z=1+u$ and assume $F_b=L/2+O(1)$. As a meromorphic \emph{resummation}
of the right-edge geometric series, the frozen ratio
$H_{\rm ex}=F_b-P+Q/(F_b-P)$ has, in a fixed normalized neighbourhood
$|u|\le C/L$, poles at $u=0$ and at one point $u=u_*(F_b)$, where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
u_*(F_b)=F_b^{-1}+O(F_b^{-2})
=\frac2L+O(L^{-2}).}
\label{eq:translated-local-pole}
\end{equation}
Their scalar residues are $-2$ and $+1$. The model source $H_{\rm mod}$ has
principal parts $-2/u+1/(u-2/L)$.

This lemma is only an algebraic locator for the translated scale. In the
actual Perron argument the quotient $Q/(F_b-P)$ is \emph{not} transported as
one meromorphic object through the Perron plane. The exact principal source is
defined by the absolutely convergent geometric expansion on the safe right
edge and by complete levelwise Laurent extraction at $u=0$; only the resulting
leading residue family is resummed. Thus $u_*$ does not appear as a separate residue term in the contour
decomposition; its role is only to identify the translated scale represented by
the resummed model.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Laurent expansion of the logarithmic derivative at the zeta pole is
\[
P(1+u)=\frac1u+p_0+O(u),
\qquad
Q(1+u)=\frac1{u^2}+O(1).
\]
Put $D(u)=F_b-P(1+u)$. Then
\[
uD(u)=F_bu-1-p_0u+O(u^2).
\]
On a circle $|u-F_b^{-1}|=C_1F_b^{-2}$ with $C_1$ fixed sufficiently large,
Rouch\'e's theorem gives exactly one zero $u_*$ of $D$ and
$u_*=F_b^{-1}+O(F_b^{-2})$. Since
\[
D'(u)=-P'(1+u)=Q(1+u),
\]
the quotient $Q/D$ has residue exactly $1$ at $u_*$. At $u=0$ the Laurent
expansions above give
\[
-P(1+u)=-\frac1u+O(1),
\qquad
\frac{Q(1+u)}{F_b-P(1+u)}=-\frac1u+O(1),
\]
while $F_b$ is analytic. Hence the frozen Perron source $H_{\rm ex}$ has
residue $-2$ at $u=0$. This proves the exact-source statement without
identifying the independent Perron variable with the original spectral
$Z_1$-function. For $H_{\rm mod}(z;F_b)=F_b-2P(z)+P(z-2/L)$ the principal parts are
immediately
\[
-\frac2u+\frac1{u-2/L}.
\]
\end{proof}
